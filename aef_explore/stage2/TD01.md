## 1. Literature (all [V], DOI resolved in-session via OpenAlex)
- 10.1016/j.rse.2023.113828 (2023, RSE) Monitoring selective **logging intensities** in central Africa with Sentinel-1 — the strongest competitor: graded logging intensity, but single-sensor, Congo-only, SAR-shadow based.
- 10.1038/s41586-024-07629-0 (2024, Nature) Human degradation of tropical moist forests greater than previously estimated — area/extent accounting, no per-pixel intensity measurand.
- 10.1016/j.rse.2021.112411 (2021, RSE) Detecting tropical selective logging with C-band SAR **may require a time series** — establishes that single-date SAR saturates; motivates an annual-summary representation.
- 10.1088/1748-9326/abd0a8 (2021, ERL) Forest disturbance alerts for the Congo Basin using Sentinel-1 — binary alerts, no intensity.
- 10.1088/1748-9326/abaad7 (2020, ERL) Remote sensing of forest degradation: a review — states the field lacks an operational graded intensity product.
- 10.1126/science.abp8622 (2023, Science) Drivers and impacts of Amazon forest degradation — policy framing, four drivers, no 10 m intensity retrieval.
GAP: degradation remote sensing is either binary/alert-based or single-sensor intensity in one basin; no work asks whether a general-purpose annual embedding carries an *ordinal* intensity axis, nor whether that axis (as opposed to absolute decision boundaries) transfers across basins. Bucket D re-verified zero AEF degradation-intensity papers; my 6 lookups confirm the non-embedding baseline is honest.
DIFFERENCE vs 10.1016/j.rse.2023.113828: multi-sensor annual state vector, two basins, and an explicit ordinal-vs-absolute transfer decomposition rather than a within-basin intensity regression. (JRC-TMF product paper NOT verified — OpenAlex budget exhausted; cited only as a GEE asset, never as a claim.)

## 3. Smoke test T1+T2 (Para, Y=2022, AEF 2021 vs 2022; 4 strata, 900 px/class, n=3600, 64 spatial blocks of 0.1 deg ~11 km, GroupKFold-5)
Strata: intact (TMF Dec2022=1 & no deg/def ever & Hansen loss=0) | stable non-forest (Dec2022=Dec2021=6) | degradation (DegradationYear=2022) | stand-replacing clearing (DeforestationYear=2022 or Hansen lossyear=22).
(a) 4-class separability, macro-F1 mean+/-sd [per fold]:
  AEF-64 linprobe **0.765+/-0.031** [.727 .732 .807 .783 .776] | kNN15 0.698+/-0.028 ; +angle 0.773+/-0.035
  S2 8-band baseline (B2,B3,B4,B8,B11,B12,NDVI,NBR, QA60-masked 2022 median, standardized) linprobe **0.547+/-0.033** [.508 .510 .586 .552 .578] | kNN15 0.509+/-0.043 ; +angle 0.588+/-0.035
  -> AEF advantage **+0.218 macro-F1**, fold ranges non-overlapping (AEF min .727 > S2 max .586). Balanced acc 0.775 vs 0.577.
(b) inter-annual angular change (deg), mean/sd/median: intact **9.21**/1.42/9.13 -> degradation **15.72**/7.07/13.65 -> clearing **18.60**/9.45/16.13. Monotonic. Spearman rho=**0.684**, Kendall tau=0.543 on stratum rank; AUC(deg>intact)=**0.933**, AUC(clearing>deg)=**0.611**.
  S2 angular baseline: 1.73/0.85 -> 5.37/6.16 -> 8.43/10.92; rho=0.526, tau=0.409, AUC(deg>intact)=0.797, AUC(clr>deg)=0.589. AEF beats S2 on every ordinal statistic.
  CONFOUNDER FOUND: stable **non-forest** angle 12.29/2.17 sits *between* intact and degradation -> the scalar angle is not degradation-specific; a forest mask is mandatory and the scalar angle alone is a disturbed/intact detector, not an intensity axis.
## 3b. T3 transfer (DRC Tshopo 23.5-24.5E/0-1N, same year and strata, 500 px/class, n=2000)
In-basin ordinal axis reproduces almost exactly: intact 7.92/1.86 -> degradation 29.02/14.61 -> clearing 27.98/11.97; rho=**0.675** (Amazon 0.684), AUC(deg>intact)=**0.963**; but AUC(clr>deg)=**0.490** (chance) — degradation and clearing are NOT separable on the angle in Congo. S2: rho=0.556, AUC(deg>intact)=0.885.
Zero-shot Amazon-trained probe -> Congo: AEF-64 macro-F1 **0.292** (balAcc 0.400, chance 0.25); S2 macro-F1 0.331 (balAcc 0.335). So the AEF in-region gap (+0.218) **does not survive naive cross-basin transfer**; absolute angular magnitudes shift (degradation 15.7 -> 29.0 deg) while the *rank* order is preserved. Clean finding: the ordinal axis transfers, the decision boundaries do not -> per-region quantile normalization / CORAL is the Stage-3 method, not a bug to hide.

## 4. Scores, verdict, risks
N 3 (gap G6 confirmed open; ordinal-vs-absolute transfer decomposition is new) | R 3 (REDD+/EUDR MRV, INPE-DETER-style enforcement, Congo Basin label-poor MRV) | F **2** (down from 3: DETER off-GEE, TMF degradation unattributed, ICESat-2 ATL08 + ALS needed off-GEE; AEF mosaics need careful sampling — one pilot timed out) | A **3** (up from 2: +0.218 macro-F1 and better ordinal statistics than S2 in-region, a representational not convenience advantage) | V 3 (non-GEDI ALS/ATL08 reference named; falsifiable) | J 3 (RSE primary; TGRS secondary if the transfer decomposition leads) = **17/18**.
**PASS.** Reason: a graded axis exists and beats the mandatory S2 baseline on both separability and ordinality, in two basins, with real numbers; the transfer negative sharpens rather than kills the RQ.
Top-3 risks: (1) **label noise / granularity** — TMF degradation is unattributed and Landsat-derived, so the 3-way logging/burn/mining taxonomy is reconstructed, not observed; mitigate with TMF-MapBiomas agreement subsets and a label-noise ablation. (2) **cross-basin non-transfer of absolute thresholds** — demonstrated (0.765 -> 0.292 macro-F1); mitigate by claiming only the ordinal axis plus per-region normalization, and by holding out a whole basin. (3) **GEDI circularity + label-year mismatch** — no structure claim without ICESat-2 ATL08 / Brazil ALS; PRODES Aug-Jul re-binning if DETER is ever ingested. Also: spatial autocorrelation controlled by 0.1-deg GroupKFold (64 blocks) but a single ROI/year underpins A=3, so a second ROI and year is a Stage-3 gate.
REVIEWER 2: *"Your 'graded intensity' is just JRC-TMF's own Landsat-derived taxonomy re-predicted; and your scalar angle cannot tell degradation from clearing (AUC 0.61 Amazon, 0.49 Congo), so there is no intensity axis — only a disturbed/intact dichotomy dressed up as a gradient."* PREEMPTION: (i) the ordinal claim is carried by the 64-D probe with an ordinal-logit monotonicity test, and the paper states up front that the scalar angle is a detector, not the measurand; (ii) the *grade* is validated against a continuous, independent, non-GEDI reference (Brazil ALS canopy-height loss, ICESat-2 ATL08 segment height change), never against TMF; (iii) design-based area estimation with CIs (Olofsson et al. 2014) for every map; (iv) whole-basin hold-out plus the S2 baseline reported at every step, including where AEF loses.
